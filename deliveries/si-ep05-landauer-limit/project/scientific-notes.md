# Scientific corrections and model boundaries

The original supplied brief is preserved in `../source/script.md`. Q approved scientific corrections in the current conversation. The story remains four acts over exactly 120 seconds; equations are evaluated numerically rather than drawn as arbitrary shapes.

1. Landauer's bound applies to resetting an initially unknown, equiprobable classical bit under the usual isothermal assumptions. It is not a universal cost of every operation or every thought. The 1961 proposal and subsequent experimental validation are distinguished. At 300 K, `k_B T ln 2 = 2.870978885 × 10^-21 J`. A trillion erasures at this lower bound produce **2.870978885 nJ**, not macroscopic scorching heat. Planet-scale totals can still be large.
2. A symmetric quartic barrier change alone does not reset a bit. The controlled potential includes a tilt term `c(t)x`. The displayed particles are a deterministic ensemble illustration, not a numerical Langevin simulation or a measurement of heat. The isothermal Szilard compression has constant thermal speed and heat `k_B T ln 2`. A separate, explicitly labeled 1D adiabatic comparison halves length, doubles speed and quadruples kinetic temperature. These two protocols must not be conflated.
3. Koomey's empirical historical fit is `computations/kWh = exp(0.440243 year - 849.259)` for the cited historical dataset. Values past that dataset are dashed extrapolation, not measurements or a forecast. The 1946 and 1971 regression values lie below the requested 10^3 main-axis floor and are explicitly labeled below range rather than clamped onto that floor. Hardware-era labels are contextual markers. The `10^16 ops/J` brain anchor is an illustrative workload convention, not a measured universal brain efficiency. `20 W` is an approximate power anchor. An erasure-per-operation assumption is required to compare an `ops/J` axis to a Landauer limit.
4. Planck's wavelength spectral radiance is in `W m^-2 sr^-1 μm^-1`; wavelength conversion includes the `10^-6` Jacobian. Wien's peak is `2897.771955/T μm`. Stefan–Boltzmann's `T^4` law describes the **integral** of exitance, not the height of an individual spectral peak. The Earth panel is a fixed effective-greybody equilibrium illustration with `R=6,371,000 m`, emissivity `0.61`, albedo `0.30`, irradiance `1361 W/m²`. It is not a climate projection. The intercepted solar power and absorbed solar power are distinguished. 345 K is a selected danger scenario; it is not the boiling point of water.
5. Sun–Earth L2 is about 1.5 million km beyond Earth. A shield, rather than an Earth umbra, provides thermal protection in the schematic. L2 does not provide a 2.725 K operating environment. The CMB is a background bath; passive rejection of nonzero heat requires a radiator warmer than that bath. A 40 K radiator example is shown separately. At 2.725 K the ideal bit bound is `2.6078 × 10^-23 J`, correctly rounded **2.61 × 10^-23**, with an ideal 110.09-fold ratio against 300 K. Refrigeration costs and finite-rate dissipation are excluded from that ideal comparison.
6. Murray's cubic branching law is a hydraulic transport model used as the array's coolant-channel geometry. It does not prove that a fractal radiator is an optimum radiative surface. Net radiative power is `εσA(T_rad^4 − T_bath^4)`. Orbital paths are labeled schematic and not to scale; a staged deployment is not a mission simulation.
7. The Fermi-paradox quotation is a speculative possibility, not an established consequence of thermodynamics. The EP06 cortex/key image is an explicitly symbolic information-security illustration.
